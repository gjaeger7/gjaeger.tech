Current state

St. George has the warmth. The new site needs structure.

The current site communicates a basic welcome, but it appears thin from the public homepage: welcome text, a parish photo, and an invitation to visit. That is a good start — but the redesign should make the parish easy to find, easy to visit, and easy to understand.

What the new site must answer fast

When are services? Can I visit? What should I expect? Where do I park? What is Orthodoxy? How do I contact someone?

Research from other Orthodox sites

What good parish websites are already doing

These examples are not about copying a design exactly. They show patterns St. George can borrow: clarity, local identity, visitor confidence, and easy parish updates.

Antiochian example

Saint Andrew Orthodox Christian Church — Riverside, CA

saintandrew.net

  • Strong positioning: “Ancient Faith. Living Community.”
  • Explicit Antiochian identity rooted in Antioch and Acts 11:26.
  • Simple service schedule and direct contact info.
  • Modern feel without becoming flashy or shallow.
Takeaway: St. George can be modern and inviting while still feeling deeply Orthodox.

Visitor pathway

Holy Cross Orthodox Church — Williamsport, PA

holycrosswilliamsport.org

  • Uses “Come and see!” language.
  • Clear “Visit Us” pathway.
  • Links to schedule, clergy, bulletins, calendar, readings, video, photos.
  • Explains Orthodoxy in a way visitors can approach.
Takeaway: build around the newcomer’s next step.

Schedule clarity

St. Dionysios Greek Orthodox Church

stdionysios.org

  • Homepage immediately shows service schedule.
  • Upcoming events are visible and current.
  • The site feels alive and useful for parishioners.
Takeaway: the homepage should answer “When is church?” in five seconds.

Local identity

Holy Theotokos of Iveron Orthodox Church — Hawaii

orthodoxhawaii.org

  • Warm local parish story.
  • Clear address, phone, email, livestream, and donation links.
  • Strong sense of place and welcome.
Takeaway: the site should feel like St. George in Kearney, not a generic church template.

Breadth of parish life

Greek Orthodox Archdiocesan Cathedral — NYC

thecathedralnyc.org

  • Clearly states jurisdiction and identity.
  • Shows services, school, youth, classes, cultural events, social services, and fellowship.
  • Communicates that a parish is more than Sunday morning.
Takeaway: even a smaller parish can present community life clearly and beautifully.

Aesthetic research

What strong Orthodox websites and media visually communicate

Instead of choosing a style arbitrarily, St. George can borrow the visual patterns that already work across Orthodox parish websites and media: reverence, clarity, warmth, local identity, and immediate practical usefulness.

Strongest overall direction

Reverent, warm, and readable

Across the better parish examples, the most successful sites do not feel like generic nonprofit pages. They use warm color, dignified typography, sacred visual cues, and simple structure so the site feels Orthodox while still being easy for a nervous visitor to use.

  • Use burgundy, gold, parchment, deep blue, and warm neutrals with restraint.
  • Pair Orthodox visual language with clean modern spacing.
  • Make service times, visitor info, and contact details impossible to miss.

Icons and sacred architecture as accents

Orthodox media often carries its identity through icons, crosses, arches, candlelight, domes, wood, gold, and liturgical texture. Those should guide the feeling of the site without turning every page into a heavy decorative frame.

  • Use icons and parish imagery as anchors, not wallpaper.
  • Repeat subtle arch shapes and Orthodox geometry.
  • Let feast days and patron-saint pages carry richer visual treatment.

Real parish life over stock imagery

The most trustworthy church sites show the actual parish: worship, coffee hour, clergy, families, candles, the building, and community life. For St. George, real photos also communicate that Orthodoxy is actually present in Central Nebraska.

  • Feature St. George people and spaces wherever possible.
  • Show the church as welcoming, active, and approachable.
  • Use local Kearney/Central Nebraska context as a strength.

Design principles pulled from the research

Clarity is pastoralVisitors should find service times, address, and “what to expect” without digging.
Beauty should inviteThe design should feel sacred and warm, not intimidating or museum-like.
Local identity mattersSt. George should feel like an Orthodox parish in Kearney, not a copied template.
Current equals aliveCalendars, bulletins, photos, and announcements signal that the parish is active.

Rural outreach vision

A better website can help St. George reach people far beyond Kearney.

Because St. George is geographically distant from the diocese and from other nearby Antiochian Orthodox parishes, the website has to do more than announce events. It can become the first point of contact for people across Central Nebraska who are curious, isolated, searching, or unsure if Orthodoxy is even available near them.

01

Be findable for rural seekers

Use clear local search language like Orthodox Church in Kearney, Orthodox Christianity Central Nebraska, Antiochian Orthodox Nebraska, and Divine Liturgy near me so people outside town can actually discover St. George.

02

Reduce the first-visit anxiety

For someone driving an hour or more, uncertainty is a major barrier. A strong Visit page can explain what to expect, where to park, when to arrive, what children can do, and how coffee hour works.

03

Serve the disconnected

Livestream links, homilies, catechism resources, bulletins, feast-day explanations, and a simple “ask Father a question” pathway can help people stay connected before they are ready or able to attend regularly.

04

Show that St. George is a regional parish

The site can name the reality plainly: St. George serves Kearney and the surrounding rural communities. That helps people understand they are not outsiders if they come from Hastings, Holdrege, Grand Island, Lexington, or a farm road in between.

The website can become a quiet missionary tool.

Not flashy. Not salesy. Just clear, beautiful, current, and easy to trust — so someone who is alone in their search for the Orthodox faith has a real next step.

Platform strategy

Choose the platform volunteers can actually use.

Since St. George already uses Tithe.ly for giving, the website platform does not need to solve donations from scratch. It needs to design well, update easily, embed Tithe.ly cleanly, and avoid locking the parish into another frustrating editor.

Recommended first prototype

Squarespace

Best balance of visual polish, simplicity, and volunteer maintainability.

  • Beautiful templates
  • Easy editing
  • Strong mobile defaults
  • Embeds Tithe.ly, Google Calendar, YouTube, forms
Most flexible

Managed WordPress

Best long-term control if someone owns maintenance or the parish pays for managed support.

  • Maximum customization
  • Strong SEO
  • Great content control
  • Needs updates/security ownership
Usable alternative

Wix

Easy drag-and-drop freedom, but can get inconsistent if many people edit casually.

  • Visual editor
  • Quick to build
  • Flexible layouts
  • Less elegant unless disciplined
Do not force it

Orthodox Web Solutions

Orthodox-specific, but currently restrictive and unintuitive for the redesign goals.

  • Orthodox context
  • Built-in parish tools
  • Restrictive editor
  • Harder to make visually excellent

Recommended platform direction

Prototype in Squarespace first.

If the group wants a beautiful, low-drama website that volunteers can update, Squarespace is the strongest first platform to test. If the group decides it needs deeper customization and has a maintenance plan, managed WordPress is the stronger long-term control option. Since St. George already uses Tithe.ly for giving, the website can simply embed or link Tithe.ly cleanly instead of replacing it.

Group plan

How to move from research to launch

01

Discovery

Review example sites, decide what feels right, define the audience, and agree on must-have pages.

02

Platform test

Try the same tasks in Squarespace, WordPress, Wix, and OWS: add event, update service time, add photo, embed calendar/video.

03

Content gathering

Collect photos, service schedule, clergy bio, parish history, Tithe.ly link, livestream links, contact info, and feast-day update process.

04

Prototype

Build Home, Visit, and Service Times first. Those pages prove the design and usability.

05

Build + launch

Finish pages, test mobile, connect domain, verify Google listing, assign maintenance owner, then launch.

Core principle

The site should not be a digital bulletin board. It should be a front door.

For St. George, the winning site will be reverent, clear, welcoming, current, and easy to maintain — a place where someone in Kearney can move from curiosity to actually walking through the doors.
