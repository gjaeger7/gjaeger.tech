The differentiator

It models passes, not just thickness.

Foam goes on in lifts, and every product has a max lift — the most it can safely be sprayed in one pass. That single fact changes the chemical, the schedule, and the price. Software that only knows square footage can’t see any of it.

Same wall. Two products. Different jobs.

A closed-cell product rated for 2″ per pass needs three passes to fill a 5.5″ wall. One rated for 4″ needs two. Passes cost chemical — the first inch of every pass yields worse — and a third pass often waits until tomorrow, which turns a one-day job into a two-day job with a second day of rig time and overhead.

Foambid Pro prices all of it: the lift plan, the extra chemical, and the extra spray day — from each product’s own max lift and cure behavior.

The catalog

Every number knows where it came from.

The pricebook ships with real products imported from manufacturers’ published technical data sheets — densities, yields, max lifts, R-values. And every field carries its provenance: a manufacturer’s published figure, an industry operating default, or an estimate. A number that can’t be traced is labeled as such — never quietly presented as fact.

Difficulty tiers

A crawlspace doesn’t bid like an open warehouse.

Every job gets scored — access, hose run, prep burden, conditions — into a tier from Green to Black. The tier carries through the whole system: labor multipliers and yield derates in the price, honest time on the schedule, and a scale for crew performance plans.

Tier 1 · Green

Walk-in access, short hose, empty building. The job the rest get measured against.

Tier 2 · Yellow

Occupied space, real masking, a longer pull. Same gun time — slower everything else.

Tier 3 · Red

Tight crawls, long hose runs, winter schedules. Productivity drops and yield follows.

Tier 4 · Black

Lifts, night work, hostile logistics. Fine work — if the bid admits what it is.

A shop that bids everything at Green wins the brutal jobs at a loss without knowing it. The rubric knows they’re harder; the tier makes sure the bid does too.

Reconciliation & traceability

Every number can be re-derived a year later.

When the work order closes, reconciliation lays the bid against what actually happened — material, hours, conditions — so the gap has an explanation instead of a shrug.

And because bids record how every input was resolved — which pricebook version, which yield figure, which difficulty rubric — you can pull up a job from last spring and reconstruct exactly why it priced the way it did. That matters for disputes, for change orders, and for finding out why a job lost money.

  • Bid vs. actual, line by line, per work order
  • Conditions from the field become the explanation, not an excuse
  • An append-only audit trail — estimates are versioned, never quietly edited

Customization

Runs the way you run.

No two foam shops price, propose, or pay the same way — and most software punishes them for it. In Foambid Pro the defaults are starting points, not rules:

  • Pricebook values — your costs, your labor rates, your rounding rules
  • Difficulty rubric — score what actually makes your jobs hard
  • Proposal templates — every word your customer reads, editable
  • Pay plan design — components, weights, and gates, per shop Design preview

Owner-defined, organization-scoped, and versioned — so a setting changed in April never rewrites what March’s jobs said.
