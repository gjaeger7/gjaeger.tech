Use case

A mock site that makes options tangible.

Most committees do not need a technical lecture. They need to see what a modern website can do: how a homepage might feel, how visitors find information, how events stay current, and how volunteers can update the site without fighting the software.

The sample should answer

What could the homepage include? How would visitors plan a visit? Can calendars and forms be simple? Where do photos and media fit? How does giving integrate? Who maintains updates?

Reference patterns

Borrow proven ideas without copying any one site.

These live examples show common website patterns worth discussing: a strong visitor pathway, clear schedules, real community photography, simple giving links, and easy-to-find contact information.

Pattern: visitor confidence

Visitor pages should reduce uncertainty.

Example reference

  • Clear “Visit Us” pathway.
  • Plain language about what to expect.
  • Easy next step for someone nervous or new.
Takeaway: the website should make the first visit feel possible.

Pattern: schedule first

People should find service times immediately.

Example reference

  • Prominent schedule.
  • Upcoming events visible.
  • Homepage feels current.
Takeaway: current information builds trust.

Pattern: local identity

Real people and real place matter.

Example reference

  • Local story.
  • Warm contact details.
  • Clear sense of community.
Takeaway: avoid generic templates when real community life is stronger.

Pattern: teaching + media

Media can serve visitors and members.

Example reference

  • Livestream or video links.
  • Homilies or teachings.
  • Photos that show parish life.
Takeaway: media makes the site useful beyond Sunday morning.

Pattern: simple giving

Giving should be obvious but not pushy.

Example reference

  • One clear Give button.
  • Works through existing provider.
  • Does not complicate the website build.
Takeaway: link or embed the tool the organization already uses.

Feature menu

Options a real site could include.

This section is intentionally broad. It lets a group point to what matters, what can wait, and what would make the website easier to maintain.

Core features

Homepage + visitor pathway

A strong homepage should answer the most important questions quickly, then guide people into deeper pages only when needed.

  • Service/event times and location.
  • Plan Your Visit button.
  • Welcome message and real photos.
  • Contact pathway for questions.

Member tools

These features make the site useful after launch instead of becoming another abandoned brochure.

  • Calendar and announcements.
  • Bulletins or newsletters.
  • Livestream/media links.
  • Email/text signup forms.

Growth options

These are optional add-ons once the core site is working well.

  • Blog/news posts.
  • Volunteer forms.
  • Event registration.
  • Resource library or FAQ.

Guiding principles

Clear beats cleverUsers should not hunt for basics.
Current equals aliveDates, photos, and announcements must stay fresh.
Real photos winAuthentic local imagery beats stock photos.
Maintenance mattersThe best site is one volunteers can actually update.

Platform strategy

Pick the platform based on who will maintain it.

This mockup can be used to compare website builders without getting lost in technical details. The real question is not just “what can it do?” — it is “who can keep it updated six months from now?”

Recommended simple prototype

Squarespace

Strong option for polished design, simple editing, and low-maintenance updates.

  • Beautiful templates
  • Easy editing
  • Strong mobile defaults
  • Embeds calendars, videos, forms, giving links
Most flexible

Managed WordPress

Best if the group wants deeper customization and has someone responsible for maintenance.

  • Maximum customization
  • Strong SEO
  • Great content control
  • Needs updates/security ownership
Quick builder

Wix

Easy drag-and-drop freedom, but needs discipline to stay clean and consistent.

  • Visual editor
  • Fast to build
  • Flexible layouts
  • Can get messy with many editors
Specialized tools

Niche Website Platforms

May include helpful built-in tools, but can be restrictive if the design/editor is weak.

  • Industry-specific features
  • May simplify donations/events
  • Can limit design quality
  • Editor quality varies

Project plan

How to move from mockup to real site.

01

Decide priorities

Use this sample to choose must-have features, nice-to-have features, and what should wait.

02

Choose platform

Compare Squarespace, managed WordPress, Wix, and niche tools based on design quality and volunteer maintenance.

03

Gather content

Collect logo, photos, schedule, contact info, donation link, leadership bios, events, and current announcements.

04

Build prototype

Build Home, Visit, Schedule, and Contact first. Those pages prove usability and design direction.

05

Launch + maintain

Test mobile, connect domain, verify search listings, train editors, and assign a recurring update owner.
